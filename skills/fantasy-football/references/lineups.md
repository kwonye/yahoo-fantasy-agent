# Lineups, bench flexibility, and IR

Read the actual scoring, roster positions, eligible positions, roster period, and lock rules. Construct a legal full roster assignment before sending its changed positions. Include both sides of swaps so transient or final duplicate slot occupancy does not invalidate the change.

Compare current-week value, role, expected workload, injury uncertainty, and replacement options. Exclude bye-week and confirmed inactive players from starting consideration when legal replacements exist. A questionable player with a late game may warrant a viable late-game fallback, not an automatic benching.

Preserve optionality: where equivalent, put earlier-playing players in their dedicated positions and later-playing eligible players in FLEX/superflex. Do not move a locked player, even to open an otherwise attractive slot. Check official inactives and the fallback plan before relevant kickoff; use the actual schedule, including early international games.

Account for QB value in superflex/2QB, reception and tight-end premiums, return-yard bonuses, and position-specific IDP scoring. Prefer stable volume for a favored lineup only when the confidence and matchup justify it; an underdog may value upside, but do not invent matchup win probabilities or chase variance at any price.

IR is a roster position whose eligibility comes from Yahoo, not from the agent's diagnosis. Check whether a returning player must be activated before Yahoo allows other moves. Prepare the full legal transition, including any required bench space or protected-player implications. A player on IR does not necessarily free an active spot when dropped.

After submission, verify selected positions for the requested week/date. An HTTP response alone is not a complete lineup check. If every legal alternative is locked or unavailable, report the remaining gap and its cause. Do not churn valuable depth to fill a low-impact slot without comparing acquisition cost and future value.
