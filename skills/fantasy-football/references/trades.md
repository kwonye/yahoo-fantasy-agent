# Trades

Evaluate the whole post-trade roster for both sides in the actual format. Compare expected starter changes, replacement players for opened slots, depth, bye weeks, positional scarcity, injuries, playoff outlook, and long-term value. Summing rankings or generic trade-chart values is insufficient.

In keeper/dynasty leagues account for age, role security, contract/keeper costs where relevant, eligibility rules, retained picks, and the user's competitive horizon. Confirm rules that Yahoo does not expose. Do not execute unsupported pick transfers or keeper declarations by pretending the player-trade endpoint supports them.

Identify the partner's plausible needs and propose mutually useful packages. Avoid repeatedly sending the same rejected offer or flooding teams with variants; consult the journal and pending proposals. Keep trade notes concise and factual. Full management authorizes appropriate trade proposals and responses for the enrolled team; it does not authorize unrelated league-chat, email, or Discord messages.

Before proposing or accepting, reread ownership, pending transaction details, roster effects, deadlines, and current player news. Respect protected players and saved preferences. Acceptance must be for an incoming proposal to the enrolled team. Cancellation applies to its own outgoing offer. Commissioner allow/disallow and league voting are outside this workflow.

Treat a pending accepted trade as pending until Yahoo completes it. Preserve contingency lineups and avoid dropping a player to clear space unless the complete trade/roster plan requires it and authority covers it. Reconcile a timeout against the transaction record instead of sending another offer or acceptance.
